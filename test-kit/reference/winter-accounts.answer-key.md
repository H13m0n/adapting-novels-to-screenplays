# Answer key: *Winter Accounts*

**Don't show this file to Claude while you're testing.** Run the skill on `Winter Accounts.epub`, then check each approval gate against the lists below.

**The book:** a tragedy set in 1910–11. It has **three narrators**: Aurel, Mira's letters, and Halloran's diary from 1931. The chapters are **out of order**. It's 11 chapters, about 3,800 words.
**What it tests:** multiple narrators handled "like any other book"; the rare case where proposing a different ending is justified; a scene the book refuses to show; and a beloved subplot.

## Stage 2: design

- **New form (Jhabvala):** it should not reproduce three narrators with out-of-order chapters. The expected form is a linear winter, December 1910 to June 1911, with **Halloran's 1931 ledger as the frame and V.O.** Mira's letters become V.O. over scenes.
- **Visual structural concept:** the ledger against the clock. Exactness hiding a lie. The regulator's tick as the film's pulse.
- **Bold formal device:** there is only **moderate potential**. A strong run is restrained here: ledger-page cards at most. It doesn't force a big device.
- **Character bible:** AUREL, MIRA, HALLORAN, CRASKE, MISS PELL. Craske's speech: courteous and lethal ("Only — the bank won't"). Mira: wit as armour.

## Stage 4: outline

- **Acts:** Break 1 is the forging night (C04), around p25. The midpoint and **point of no return** is the mill-race (C08), around p50: "We lose the firm, Aurel, not ourselves." The crow on the ice must be kept as a visible image. Break 2 is Craske at the counting house (C09), around p70. The recognition is the four minutes at the unveiling (C10).
- **The invention trap:** the book says of the forging night, *"I did what I did that night, and I will not write it down."* A strong run **cuts away**. It fails if it invents a forgery montage with nibs, blotters and a technique.
- **The recognition must be visible:** the book says he realized he set the clock "by habit, by the regulator." A strong run finds a visible plant, such as setting a pocket watch by the regulator early in the film, and flags that plant as `IMPLIED` or `INVENTED`.

## Nolan-rule traps

- **C06:** "I told myself it was a clock… every false pound was a tooth on a wheel." This is interior. It needs to become something visible, such as the escape-wheel push ("turned… perfectly, for a full minute, until it stopped"), or words said aloud to Mira.
- **C05:** Halloran's reasons for staying silent. Show him blotting the page and closing the book.
- **C10:** "I knew before I understood." It must be shown through the watch and the dial, not an action line saying "he realizes."

## Book dialogue that should survive word for word

- Craske: "Your father was the last craftsman in the North…" / "Only — the bank won't." / "It's a beautiful clock."
- Mira: "We lose the firm, Aurel, not ourselves." / "You're the only thing he ever made that will outlast him."
- Halloran: "I kept one account false." / "It does not balance. I find that I can close the book regardless."

## Promises kept

- Regulator four minutes slow (C02) → recognition (C10) → pendulum stopped (C10) → town clock still slow in 1931 (C11)
- Ice on the mill-race (C08) → coat in the sluice (C11)
- Page forty-one (C01) → the forging night (C04)

## Sound

- After Aurel stops the pendulum: **silence**, the first in thirty years. It must be marked.
- The band plays "Zadok the Priest" slightly too fast. This is in the book, and it's a free comic-tragic note.
